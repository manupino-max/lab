## Five new issues detected in this checkpoint

### N-M2-1 — Canonical execution is environment-blocked

The public runner is now canonical, but the current execution environment lacks `concept_erasure`. This prevents claiming a fresh numerical result. The next execution should occur in the intended LAB dependency environment, not by replacing LEACE with a hand-written substitute.

### N-M2-2 — The M* scope requires source-of-truth confirmation before promotion

The public M4 runner cannot itself establish that it is the scientific M2 protocol. M* must confirm the M2 commit/protocol before any M4 output is promoted as M2 evidence.

### N-M2-3 — Three-way evaluation should be explicit

Recent work on concept representations evaluates concept-space estimation, probing, and test data on disjoint partitions. This strengthens the M2 requirement beyond simply saying “held-out test”: the scientific protocol should explicitly distinguish representation-fitting data, probe-fitting data, and final test data where feasible.

### N-M2-4 — Nonlinear probe is a robustness check, not proof of independence

A post-intervention nonlinear AUC near chance is useful evidence but does not establish universal statistical independence. The draft must avoid upgrading probe results into stronger claims than the protocol supports.

### N-M2-5 — Utility must be treated as a co-primary consequence of intervention

Recent concept-erasure literature explicitly studies the trade-off between erasure and retained utility. Therefore M2 should not report leakage reduction without the corresponding utility delta and uncertainty.

## Literature comparison relevant to M2

- Belrose et al. (NeurIPS 2023) define LEACE as a closed-form method with guarantees against **linear** concept detection, not universal nonlinear independence. This supports retaining the distinction between linear erasure and broader leakage claims.
- Chowdhury et al. (AISTATS 2025) formalize fundamental limits/trade-offs between concept erasure and retained utility. This supports treating utility degradation as a central outcome rather than a secondary metric.
- A 2026 CoNLL study evaluates concept-subspace estimators with three disjoint partitions (space-train, probe-train, test) specifically to assess generalization of both the estimator and probes. This supports strengthening M2's held-out design where computationally feasible.
